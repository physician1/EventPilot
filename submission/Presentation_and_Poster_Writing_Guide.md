Team/project: EventPilot. Members: Enock Okorno Ayiku and Haley Wodarczyk.

This is writing and layout guidance, not a finished slide deck. The assignment prohibits AI-generated slides. Build slides manually in Google Slides or Canva without AI mode. Use your actual team photos and screenshots. Keep three ideas throughout: clarify the brief, research options, keep the human in control. Suggested speaker assignments below are optional.

## Seven-minute presentation storyboard

1. 0:00-0:35 | Enock | “EventPilot: from a brief to a plan you can question.” Team name, both names and authentic photos. Open with: “Imagine organizing a birthday in Accra for 40 guests. You know the budget, but the research is scattered.”
2. 0:35-1:20 | Enock | Problem and target users. Show the journey from separate browser tabs and notes to one saved brief. Say: “We focus on individuals and small teams. This audience is a hypothesis we still need to validate through interviews.”
3. 1:20-2:00 | Haley | Alternatives. Compare Cvent Vendor Marketplace, Peerspace and manual search/spreadsheets. Cvent already offers AI-assisted vendor matching; avoid claiming we invented AI sourcing. Explain our prototype's focus on a revisable research-to-planning view. Put source links in notes and references.
4. 2:00-2:50 | Haley | Workflow. Manually draw five steps: saved brief, consent, research agent plus web search, cited results, human review. Label live research “single agent.” Show rehearsal separately as fictional Boston data. Do not put a critic in the live diagram.
5. 2:50-4:20 | Enock | Core demo. Show authentic screens: saved Accra requirements, Agent studio citations, Overview allocations and Timeline. Open one source. Say: “These budget numbers allocate the spending limit; they are not supplier quotes.” If showing a recorded example, label it with its date. Do not claim provider connectivity from a recording.
6. 4:20-5:15 | Haley | Evidence and responsible use. Show three facts: 61 automated checks passed; production build passed; supplier facts and prices still need confirmation. Explain that mocked tests are not live accuracy benchmarks. Mention consent, bounded calls, source links and no booking or payment actions.
7. 5:15-6:20 | Enock | Value and future. Describe unified context and transparent uncertainty as the proposed value, not proven market superiority. Priorities: structured evidence, quote-aware budgeting, then cross-location evaluation. Explain how earlier Boston-only and empty-dashboard failures informed the design.
8. 6:20-7:00 | Both | Close and Q&A. “EventPilot helps people assemble a starting plan while keeping the decisions with them.” Show GitHub link/QR only after it exists. Invite questions. Keep references and technical details in untimed appendix slides.

## Horizontal one-slide poster

Manually create a landscape slide. Suggested reading order: title/team at top; problem and audience on left; one authentic app screenshot and simple workflow centrally; evidence and limits on right; compact sources and acknowledgments at bottom. Use short text and generous space, not the full report. Insert a real GitHub QR after publishing.

## Two-minute speaking script

“Imagine planning a birthday in Accra for 40 people with a five-thousand-dollar budget. The challenge is not just finding links. It is turning scattered information into a plan you can review and change.

We are EventPilot: Enock Okorno Ayiku and Haley Wodarczyk. Our prototype is intended for people planning celebrations and small teams arranging gatherings.

You save the location, date, guest count and budget. After your consent, a research agent uses web search to produce a shortlist with sources. EventPilot then presents provisional suppliers, budget allocations and a timeline. You can inspect a source, change your preferences and research again.

The distinction is important: an allocation is not a supplier quote. A venue appearing in search is not confirmed available. The person remains responsible for verifying and deciding, and the app sends no bookings or payments.

Existing alternatives include Cvent, Peerspace and manual search with spreadsheets. Our focus is a clear, revisable starting plan that keeps the brief and the evidence together.

We passed 55 backend checks and six frontend checks, and the production build succeeded. These are software checks, not proof of worldwide supplier accuracy. Earlier failures showed why we need clearer mode labels, reliable research formatting and explicit uncertainty.

Our next steps are structured evidence, quote-aware budgets and evaluation across more locations. EventPilot helps people start planning with sources while keeping the final decisions in their hands. Thank you.”

Rehearse aloud and trim to two minutes at your own speaking pace.

## Likely questions

- Is this multi-agent? Live research is one agent with web search; the separate catalog implementation has planner/critic roles.
- Does it work worldwide? No city allowlist in live mode; coverage and quality vary. The fictional rehearsal only supports Boston.
- Are the costs real? The live dashboard starts with policy allocations, not verified quotes. Local currency is not converted automatically.
- What did you test? 61 automated checks and a successful build; live relevance needs a larger human-scored evaluation.
- What is unique? The proposed workflow combines saved context, cited research and editable allocations. We have not proven a defensible advantage over commercial products.
- Can it book? No. It does not book, pay or contact suppliers.
